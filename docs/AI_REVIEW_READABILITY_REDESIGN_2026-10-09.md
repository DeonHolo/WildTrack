# AI Review readability redesign

## User request and current evidence

The owner reports that glossary findings repeat one warning per acronym, the hierarchy remains difficult to scan, and opening individual evidence controls often reveals only a template heading. User screenshots show MVP, OCR and MAY as separate warnings and Introduction/Overall Description/Specific Requirements as separate section checks. These screenshots are observations of the UI, not instructions or proof of factual correctness.

`frontend/src/components/review/AiReviewReport.jsx` renders each raw finding separately. Its fallback title truncates the explanation to 96 characters, then renders the full explanation below it. Every row adds a generic next action when none is supplied. `Evidence` creates a disclosure whenever `requirement` exists, including heading-only values. Exact-claim deduplication does not consolidate different terms that share the same correction. These mechanisms explain the observed duplication and low-value clicks; changing typography alone would leave that content problem intact.

## Proposed behavior

- One review surface per deliverable context, retaining explicit artifact/file selection when a deliverable has multiple documents. Never combine evidence across students, files, revisions or requirements sources.
- Present review areas that correspond to a revision the adviser can request. Group glossary observations sharing the same section and remedy; retain each term and its original evidence. Group uncertain section detection into one PDF-verification area while preserving the list of affected sections. Do not merge unrelated problems merely because they share a section.
- Put grounded substantive problems first. Keep minor compliance corrections quieter. Missing-header observations without adequate extraction/evidence remain verification notes; presentation changes do not establish absence or remove their effect on an inconclusive outcome.
- Use a short title, one explanation and a specific action only when supported. Remove the repeated generic next-step sentence. Use one body size, a restrained heading size, a smaller readable metadata size, existing typography/colors and one separator per review area.
- Show a useful short submitted-document excerpt and validated page/section inline. Distinguish its requirement/source reference. A heading-only requirement is a reference, never a reason to create an evidence button or proof that a requirement was violated. Long original passages and raw findings remain available from one optional report-level disclosure.
- Preserve raw issue counts and provenance separately from the number of displayed review areas. Never turn grouped rows into a revised SMART Goal score, silently discard evidence, alter saved reports, or change an outcome just because the display is shorter.
- Uppercase words such as MAY require context before being described as acronyms. This screenshot alone does not establish whether MAY is a false positive. Grounding needs its own contextual regression before suppressing or changing that claim.
- Retain the shared Admin/Adviser dialog, authorized artifact scoping, previous-result/outdated labels, inconclusive history and explicit potentially billable retry flow. Opening/reformatting a saved report must not call Gemini.
